This project predicts coastal water levels ahead of time from ocean data and provides warning for New York Harbor and the Los Angeles area. It combines two complementary approaches.

First, the remote ocean gives seasonal warning months ahead. For every October–April winter from 1980 to 2025, we counted tide-removed storm-surge days at New York's Battery tide gauge and high-water days in Los Angeles, then screened 29 NASA and NOAA clues, from sea height, temperature and winds to sea ice and river flow, at a 10% false-discovery rate on 1981–2010. Four passed; two also predicted 2011–2025 and every left-out winter, and both measure El Niño. June Niño-3.4, read four months before the season, raises storm-surge days ×1.64 per standard deviation in New York (p < 0.0001) and high-water days ×3.5 per standard deviation in Los Angeles. Strong El Niño winters bring New York 16 storm-surge days against 10 in an average winter, and the count model's leave-one-winter-out error is 3.8 days per winter against 4.6 for a trend-only model; last winter's own count adds no skill. The 2026–27 outlook for New York is about 21 storm-surge days (likely 14–32), about twice normal.

Second, ocean physics gives finer-scale 14-day forecasts. A nonlinear equatorial shallow-water model forecasts the dynamical correction to the observed background, so persistence is the exact reference. Using NASA SSH and OSCAR surface currents with no future wind forcing, its 182 chronologically validated forecasts over 2023–2026 have 12.3% lower sea-height RMSE than persistence (95% CI +10.3% to +14.3%); 168 of 182 (92%) beat persistence, and 14-day surface-current skill improves by 9.4%.

An interactive website (https://ivyreia3-boop.github.io/wbsite/sea-level-detective/) presents both parts with a 3D globe, an East/West coast switch, a "Test a winter" replay and charts. It loads live NASA GIBS satellite imagery and shows results precomputed from records through October 2026.

Together, the project links global Earth observations, statistical learning and physical ocean modelling to provide both seasonal early warning and 14-day dynamical prediction.
